# RESP closeout: contribution refunds, returned benefits and AIP tax

This English article connects four editable original scenarios to a confirmed
hypothetical RESP closing ledger. Four official CRA/Canada.ca HTML sources were
checked and retained with retrieval dates and SHA-256 hashes under the ignored
`qa/resp-aip-next/` directory. No private account, client, email, archived PDF
or private knowledge index was used.

The default composition is CAD 32,000 of refundable subscriber contributions,
CAD 6,400 of benefits already confirmed repayable to government, and CAD
26,000 of eligible AIP. Total: CAD 64,400. These components are not inferred
from a balance or contribution history. The hypothetical promoter has already
confirmed their amounts, permitted payment and eligibility. The example assumes
an original subscriber who meets the selected Canadian-resident, sole-payee,
plan anniversary and beneficiary eligibility route in the current CRA guidance.
It makes no determination for any real person, RESP contract or payment.

The verified subscriber has CAD 15,000 of available tax-year deduction capacity
and has used CAD 40,000 of the separate CAD 50,000 lifetime AIP-related tax
limit. The modeled deduction is the minimum of confirmed eligible AIP, current
deduction capacity and remaining lifetime ceiling: CAD 10,000. The example
assumes a 30% incremental regular-tax rate and 20% additional tax outside
Quebec. It excludes Quebec’s 12% additional rate and all personal tax returns.

With no qualifying deduction, CAD 26,000 remains subject to the assumed rates:
CAD 7,800 regular plus CAD 5,200 additional tax. Subscriber cash is CAD
45,000 after current illustrative tax. With the maximum modeled CAD 10,000
deduction, remaining AIP is CAD 16,000, illustrative tax totals CAD 8,000, cash
is CAD 40,000 and the retirement account holds CAD 10,000 before future tax.
Both allocations also return CAD 6,400 benefits and reconcile to the same
CAD 64,400 balance. This is a present-cash/destination illustration, not a
permanent tax-saving, payment-date, withholding, refund or future-RRSP-tax
estimate.

Four figures show the starting composition, both reconciled allocations, the
two declining tax amounts against eligible deduction, and a matrix that changes
current deduction capacity independently from prior lifetime-limit use. The
matrix holds the entered AIP at CAD 26,000. It never authorizes amounts above
the selected AIP or verified limits. The end-February termination deadline
after the first AIP is disclosed, but no calendar is calculated.

Ten focused tests cover dollar and cent reconciliation, no-deduction and
maximum-deduction outcomes, the separate capacity/lifetime/AIP limits, zero
tax-rate cases, tax-curve values, matrix checkpoints, half-cent rounding,
control boundaries, invalid amounts, accessible charts, four distinct series
colours, public-source references, article length and related-reading routes.
