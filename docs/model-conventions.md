# TickerLantern annual model conventions

Version: `tickerlantern-annual-forward-anchor-v2` · reviewed 23 October 2026.
Machine-readable policy: [`data/model-conventions.json`](../data/model-conventions.json).
Executing model: [`src/model.js`](../src/model.js).

The calculation engine is an educational annual scenario. Published inputs can inform it, but it does not reproduce an official index, an official EPS series or an investor's realized return. All tables, charts, sensitivity cases and annual CSV values must come from the same accepted engine output. The source/definition registry separately determines which observations may supply scenario defaults.

## Earnings and valuation timeline

Starting wealth is 100 normalized units and the investor owns one unit. At entry, `F0 = 100 / entryPE` is a **synthetic normalized forward earnings anchor**. It is not an independently observed official index EPS value.

For the illustrative rolling-NTM interpretation, F0 represents approximately the first holding year's earnings. At valuation date t, `Ft = F(t−1) × (1 + gt)` represents earnings over the following period, approximately holding year t+1. At exit after n years, `Pn = exitPE × Fn`. Exactly n growth factors are applied. Starting from a forward anchor does not justify an extra growth factor.

The engine accepts distinct annual growth assumptions. The workbench uses [timing-v2](five-year-forecast-policy.md): explicit CY/rolling12m sources may supply a modeled uniform-accrual calendar-to-forward bridge; otherwise each of the five years uses a benchmark-specific editorial normalized EPS assumption. Raw publisher figures stay separately visible. No generic FY/CY/publisher-endpoint conversion is presumed. All resulting holding-year rates remain conditional model inputs.

The intermediate valuation path is `Mt = entryPE × (exitPE / entryPE)^(t/n)`. This positive geometric interpolation is an assumption. It influences interim prices and reinvestment; the terminal price still follows the chosen exit multiple and cumulative earnings path.

## Distributions, cash and reinvestment

In payout mode, year t's per-original-unit dividend is `Dt = payout × F(t−1)`. The dividend therefore uses the anchor at the start of the holding year and is paid at year end. In yield mode, the initial yield sets only the first payment: `D1 = 100 × yield`; later payments scale with the previous earnings anchor. This subsequent dividend-growth rule is a scenario assumption, not a source forecast. Source yield methodology, including trailing versus indicated yield and gross/net treatment, must remain separately disclosed.

Prices are modeled as ex-distribution values. Do not subtract the dividend again. Cash-held wealth is `Pt + sum(D1…Dt)` with **zero interest on cash**. Periodic investor cash flows are `[-100, D1, …, Dn + Pn]`; the final dividend and sale occur once each.

Reinvestment applies each year's dividend to the current unit balance, including previously reinvested units, and buys additional fractional units at that same year-end modeled ex-distribution price: `Qt = Q(t−1) × (1 + Dt/Pt)`. Reinvested wealth is `Qt × Pt`. This annual convention does not replicate an official index's daily ex-date dividend treatment. Reinvestment can underperform holding cash in a falling market.

Growth and payout must be economically feasible together. The model does not infer feasibility from retention/ROE without supporting inputs. It does not infer a reported payout ratio from unmatched forward P/E and dividend yield. The [benchmark dividend policy](dividend-policy.md) instead calibrates a clearly labelled model ratio to reproduce the benchmark’s initial dividend anchor: `payout percent = dividend-anchor percent × active entry PE`. Later dividends follow the chosen EPS path. This keeps source dividend definitions and dates separate from the synthetic earnings anchor. Per-share earnings growth may already incorporate buybacks; no additional buyback yield is added.

## Return measures and attribution

| Measure | Definition and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Price CAGR | `(Pn / 100)^(1/n) − 1`, excluding all distributions. |
| Cash-flow IRR | Unique periodic rate making the present value of `[-100, D1, …, Dn+Pn]` zero. It recognizes payment timing and does not assume cash is reinvested at the IRR. |
| Reinvested CAGR | `(Qn × Pn / 100)^(1/n) − 1`, following the stated reinvestment prices. |
| Cash-held wealth CAGR | `((Pn + sum(Dt))/100)^(1/n) − 1`; the annualized equivalent of zero-interest cash-held terminal wealth, distinct from cash-flow IRR. |
| Equivalent EPS CAGR | `(Fn/F0)^(1/n) − 1`, describing the complete chosen yearly path. |

Sequential attribution reconciles `100 + EPS-only gain + valuation impact + cash dividends` to cash-held terminal wealth. These components are cumulative normalized amounts, **not additive annualized returns**. This order assigns the earnings/valuation interaction to valuation; it is an explanatory choice rather than a unique economic decomposition.

Returns are nominal, before taxes, withholding, fees and trading costs. Wealth is normalized and currency-neutral: no FX translation, hedging, inflation adjustment or CAD-investor return is modeled. A currency amount used to scale an educational example must not imply that those effects have been included.

## Defaults, scenarios and exports

The fixed opening illustration uses 6% growth, 25× entry P/E, 20× exit P/E, 30% payout and five years. These values are illustrative assumptions, not current source data. The 1.2% yield default is equivalent to a 30% payout at a 25× synthetic entry anchor under this specific timing; it is not a general identity between arbitrary published yield and P/E observations. Independently source-linked scenarios use dated per-metric evidence and preserve protected reader edits. Historical exit defaults require the compatibility policy; a historical mean is not a fair-value forecast.

The sensitivity grid uses alternative constant growth rates and exit multiples while preserving the active horizon and dividend convention. It includes lower, unchanged and higher entry-relative multiples where calculator bounds allow. It does not assign probabilities. Selecting a cell keeps its axes stable. A nonconstant annual path remains explicitly separate from constant-rate grid cases.

The workbench fixes all scenarios at five years; the generic calculation engine retains other horizons for verification. The supported calculator domain is growth −99% to 1,000% per holding year, entry/exit P/E 1–100×, payout 0–100%, initial yield 0–15%, and 1–20 whole years. These are calculator bounds; source observations must never be clipped or recast as zero to fit them. Values outside the supported domain require an explicit unavailable model input or reader assumption.

CSV uses full round-trip numeric precision, preserving tiny nonzero modeled anchors. It preserves the annual table's accepted results and includes the convention version, assumptions, actual yearly rates, source/custom growth labels, available source provenance and snapshot context supplied by the caller. Absent context is blank; it is never manufactured. CSV adds calculated/unavailable status and reasons while growth remains the final column for existing integrations. Rows represent entry and annual valuation dates, not publisher fiscal statement dates.

## Independent verification

`tests/model-audit.test.mjs` derives checks independently of the engine:

- Constant P/E makes price CAGR equal the constant earnings-growth assumption; zero dividends make all return measures coincide.
- One year, 20× to 20×, 6% growth and a 30% prior-anchor payout gives price 106, dividend 1.5 and investor return 7.5%.
- Five years, 25× to 20×, 6% growth and 30% payout gives approximately 1.373365% price CAGR and 2.686506% cash-flow IRR.
- With 20× to 5× over two years, zero growth and 100% payout, prices are 100, 50, 25; dividends are 5, 5; cash flows are −100, 5, 30. Cash-held terminal wealth is 35. Reinvested units are 1.32 and wealth 33. Price CAGR is −50%, IRR approximately −42.6707195%, reinvested CAGR approximately −42.5543735%.
- The 30% payout and 1.2% initial yield at 25× produce identical distributions under matching timing, including a changing annual earnings path.
- A 1,536-case grid covers supported growth/valuation/payout/horizon extremes and independently discounts every projected cash-flow sequence to check the IRR residual relative to initial wealth.

The solver brackets and bisects `log(1+r)` using log-sum-exp present values. It reports iterations and a scale-relative NPV residual for the returned rounded rate. The required residual is at most `1e−10` of initial wealth. Invalid cash flows, all-zero future proceeds, unrepresentable finite rates or failed convergence are rejected. The prior solver's arbitrary negative-rate floor and positive-rate cap could fabricate results for direct calls outside projected scenario ranges; these are removed. No arithmetic defect in the baseline supported model formulas was found.
