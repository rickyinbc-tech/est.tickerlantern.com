The multiple can overpower growth.

Price appreciation combines earnings growth and a change in valuation. The exact annualized relationship is:

1 + price CAGR =
(1 + EPS CAGR) × (exit P/E ÷ entry P/E)1/n

“EPS growth + multiple change + dividend yield” is a useful shorthand. It is an approximation; dividend timing and compounding matter.

A buyback effect already embedded in EPS growth should not be added a second time as a separate return yield.

The investment lens

Growing profits do not guarantee
the same growth in your investment.

If you estimate a future index level using future EPS × exit P/E, you have projected a future price. Dividends received along the way belong in the investor’s return calculation.

The apparent contradiction disappears when we distinguish the company’s earnings from the investor’s cash flows. EPS measures profit per share; EPS growth measures the change in that profit. Neither is, by itself, the shareholder’s total return.

A good return estimate therefore asks three separate questions: how quickly will earnings grow, what multiple will investors pay at exit, and how much cash will owners receive?

Accounting foundation: SEC financial-statement guide [1]. Index conventions: S&P Dow Jones Indices [3].

“If dividends come out of earnings, why add them to return?”

Because you receive both the cash paid during your holding period and the value of what you sell at the end.

1

Profit is measured before ordinary dividends.

A cash dividend to common shareholders distributes value. It is not an operating expense that reduces that period’s EPS. [1]

2

Growth is the change in future earning power.

If EPS rises from $10.00 to $10.60, earnings grew 6%. That $0.60 increase does not limit the company to paying a $0.60 dividend.

3

Retained capital and payout must reconcile.

Less retained capital can mean less investment capacity. Do not assume growth funded by 100% retention while also distributing 30% of the same earnings.

⇄

Value changes location.

A $200 share paying a $3 dividend would, all else equal, trade near $197 when it goes ex-dividend. You then own roughly $197 of shares and $3 of cash. The payment itself creates no instant gain. [2]

The exit P/E question

A historical average is an anchor.
It is not a valuation verdict.

μ

Mean versus median

The mean adds every observation and divides by the count. The median is the middle observation. Extreme valuations can pull the mean away from the median.

Neither is automatically the right exit multiple. A “10-year average forward P/E” cannot be relabelled a median without the underlying series.

f

Keep the earnings basis consistent

Forward, trailing, reported and operating earnings are different denominators. Use the same definition at entry and exit.

For example, an exit next-twelve-month P/E must multiply next-twelve-month EPS as of the exit date, rather than the wrong fiscal year’s earnings.

↔

Test a range, then explain it

Rates, profitability, sector mix, growth durability and investor risk appetite can change the multiple the market supports.

Use historical data to inform a range, then test the return across that range. A precise-looking median does not remove terminal-value uncertainty.

Model conventions and formulas

The initial price is normalized to 100. Initial earnings E₀ = 100 ÷ entry P/E is a synthetic anchor, not an independently verified official index EPS series. Under an illustrative rolling-NTM interpretation, E₀ covers approximately the first holding year; Eₜ at valuation date t covers the following earnings period. After n years the exit multiple applies to Eₙ, approximately holding year n + 1. Exactly n growth factors are applied; no extra growth year is added. FY-based or unspecified publisher multiples and annual growth estimates are retained only as qualified synthetic scenario inputs, with their period definitions visible. They do not establish a date-matched NTM forecast.

  • EPS path: Eₜ = Eₜ₋₁ × (1 + gₜ), using the selected yearly rate or one constant rate.
  • Valuation path: Mₜ = M₀ × (Mₙ ÷ M₀)ᵗ⁄ⁿ.
  • Ex-distribution price: Pₜ = Eₜ × Mₜ.
  • Dividends in payout mode: Dₜ = payout × Eₜ₋₁. In yield mode: Dₜ = 100 × initial yield × Eₜ₋₁/E₀. Payments occur at year-end.
  • Cash-flow IRR solves 0 = −100 + Σ Dₜ/(1+r)ᵗ + Pₙ/(1+r)ⁿ.
  • Reinvested units: q₀ = 1; qₜ = qₜ₋₁ × (1 + Dₜ/Pₜ).
  • Reinvested CAGR = (qₙ × Pₙ/100)¹⁄ⁿ − 1.
  • Cash-held wealth = Pₜ + Σ Dᵢ; idle cash earns 0%.

In payout mode, dividends use the prior valuation date’s EPS anchor, not the ending year’s EPS. In yield mode, the source’s historical dividend yield anchors the first annual payment; that is a scenario convention, not a dividend forecast. This reproduces the discussion’s $3 dividend on a $10 initial EPS at a 30% payout. Real dividends, EPS and prices do not follow these smooth paths.

The terminal price is treated as already reflecting distributions. The model does not claim dividends create free value; growth and valuation must be plausible under the selected payout policy.

IRR, CAGR and dividend timing

Price CAGR annualizes the change in the original unit’s price. Cash-flow IRR uses a negative initial purchase, annual dividends, and a final sale. It captures when the cash arrives; it is not obtained by simply adding today’s yield to a multi-year price CAGR.

Reinvested CAGR measures terminal wealth when every dividend purchases more units at the assumed year-end price. It depends on the intermediate price path. It can differ from cash-flow IRR. Cash-held wealth includes uninvested dividends; its terminal-wealth CAGR is a third, distinct measure.

Scope, provenance and limitations

This report develops a discussion about S&P 500 return estimation and the treatment of dividends. The original ChatGPT estimate’s answer text and attached audit workbook could not be retrieved in a usable form. The publication does not reproduce or validate those numerical estimates.

The five-year $200 worked example extends the dividend mechanics using 6% annual growth and a 30% prior-period EPS payout. The index explorer uses dated publisher observations with daily source checks. Entry and available historical exit valuations can follow those observations. Published annual earnings estimates can follow sources; custom growth remains a reader assumption and all holding periods are five years; no complete ten-year forward-P/E history or intraday price feed is used.

This is an educational earnings-multiple scenario framework, not a validated DCF or an investment recommendation. It excludes tax, withholding, fees, tracking error, transaction costs, inflation, FX, changing index membership, dilution and separately modelled buybacks. The model does not enforce a balance-sheet or sustainable-growth constraint.

Returns are nominal normalized scenarios before additional investor taxes and fees. A source yield retains its disclosed or unknown gross/net construction. A Canadian investor’s realized CAD return can differ with currency movements and account treatment. No personal account information, raw chat transcript or private source attachment is published.

To turn the illustration into a forecast

Verify the entry index level and earnings denominator as of a specific date; use a documented EPS path; obtain an actual historical forward-P/E series; justify the exit range; and model the dividend schedule.

Recheck payout feasibility, buyback assumptions and the share-count bridge before interpreting the result as investable expected return.

Index data and automatic updates

The index desk contains 21 distinct benchmarks. Connected valuation observations come from FactSet, MSCI, J.P. Morgan, Nasdaq, LSEG and other named public research publishers. Current multiples, historical means and dividend yields retain separate sources and dates when they differ. The S&P 500 ten-year mean is the publisher-reported average of historical forward multiples; its underlying sampling frequency is not specified in that article. No ten-year median or complete historical series is reconstructed.

Configured automated source adapters run daily at 23:15 UTC. Seven dividend references require separate manual review; they are not covered by that scheduled job. Annual earnings forecasts are checked in a separate refresh job. Public research feeds, sitemaps and publisher listings discover replacement reports where supported; the dated CSI 300 article is checked for revisions only. Consensus and house forecasts remain distinct. A new validated snapshot is published to GitHub. Sources include rolling documents, research feeds and older dated references. Fixed older reports are checked for revisions; a successful check does not turn them into a current rolling ten-year window. Open pages check for new snapshots every five minutes while visible, and when the page becomes visible again. The update button checks the latest published snapshot; it does not force a publisher to produce new data. Nasdaq and Edelweiss reject the hosted server, so their reports also have a scheduled browser check at 1 p.m. Vancouver time. That check requires the owner’s Mac and Codex to be available. Its independent verification time is preserved; checks older than 48 hours are flagged as overdue and the last figures are retained.

Source observation dates are distinct from source-check timestamps. Failed requests, changed table formats or mismatched index names retain the prior verified data with a visible retained status. An older observation never overwrites a newer metric. A new current multiple does not erase an older independently sourced historical mean, and a median is never accepted as a ten-year average. Unconnected metrics stay blank. A monthly observation remains monthly even if it is checked every day.

Source following updates eligible entry valuations, available ten-year reference multiples and the initial-yield input. A dated reference keeps its original observation date; undisclosed window endpoints remain unknown; it is not a current fair-value estimate. Direct premium/discount statistics require a documented compatibility assessment. A verified same-index ten-year forward mean can also supply an explicit historical exit assumption when dated or cross-source; its qualifications remain visible. If no forward reference is usable, a verified exact-index ten-year TTM mean is paired with current TTM earnings for the current reference and projected terminal trailing earnings for the exit. The latter is converted into an equivalent forward exit multiple to preserve the model’s earnings basis. Edited fields are protected per benchmark. Source-informed growth mode uses five holding years dated to the recorded valuation input. All 21 benchmarks use published forecasts only in their named years and sourced long-term EPS projections in missing years. Consensus and house forecasts retain their original period labels. The workbench names each long-term market/region proxy, its dated source and calculation. No near-term growth rate is capped, faded or carried forward. These rates are applied to synthetic forward earnings as scenario assumptions; no matched rolling-NTM series is claimed. Source following preserves custom yearly growth. Old saved source defaults migrate to this shared policy, while explicit user edits and custom growth modes remain protected. If neither a forward reference nor a verified matched TTM fallback exists, the illustrative exit follows the active entry P/E until you edit it. An eligible historical exit is an illustrative mean-reversion assumption, not a fair-value estimate.
