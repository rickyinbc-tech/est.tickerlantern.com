# Affordable contributions and distribution-tax cash

Two English articles dated and reviewed 25 October 2026, each with four editable figures and chart data tables:

- `dollar-cost-averaging-income-interruption-cash-floor` (`dcaBudget`): regular purchases inside a household cash budget, income interruption, a purchase floor, sales for essentials and unfunded spending. Supplements the existing DCA unit-cost, sequence and market-timing guides.
- `reinvested-fund-distribution-tax-cash-provision` (`fundTaxCash`): reinvestment versus retained cash, a hypothetical distribution-tax provision and dedicated outside cash. Supplements the existing fund distribution/NAV and cost-base guides; it does not reproduce their tax-basis calculation.

Public primary HTML sources reviewed: FINRA DCA, published 19 May 2026; FCAC emergency-fund guidance; OSC risk capacity/tolerance, updated 5 September 2025; CRA tax treatment of mutual funds. Source URLs and scope are linked in each article. All return paths, provision factors, income amounts, cash floors and valuations are hypothetical. No official market statistics or historical crisis observations are implied. No actual Canadian tax rate, inclusion rate, personal eligibility or tax liability is calculated.

The budget model applies the current investment return, receives income, pays essentials, sells available investment for a cash shortfall, records any unfunded expense, and only then purchases up to the desired amount from cash above a selected floor. The comparison uses the same resources and a zero floor. The floor limits new purchases but does not preserve a guaranteed cash balance. No borrowing, fees, tax, cash interest, inflation or settlement delay is included. The adaptive purchase schedule is explicitly distinguished from strict equal-amount DCA.

Default selected-floor totals: transfers CAD 3,200, sales CAD 0, unfunded essentials CAD 0, ending investment CAD 23,542.77309555160 and cash CAD 3,900. Zero-floor totals: transfers CAD 5,500, sales CAD 500, ending investment CAD 25,524.94142077905 and cash CAD 2,100. Independent 40-digit Python Decimal calculations use a fractional-unit ledger. The selected-floor rule has more cash but lower ending total resources in this example; liquidity protection is not presented as a free return improvement. With zero market returns, both finish at CAD 27,100, equal to starting resources plus income less actual spending.

The distribution model starts with 500 units at CAD 21 before a CAD 1 per-unit distribution. Post-event NAV is CAD 20. Full reinvestment adds 25 units, leaving CAD 10,500 in the fund. A hypothetical 30% provision on the CAD 500 gross distribution creates a CAD 150 liability and cash gap when outside cash is zero. Retaining CAD 150 and reinvesting CAD 350 adds 17.5 units and funds the provision; both allocations have immediate resources net of the modeled liability of CAD 10,350. Entering CAD 150 of outside cash increases starting resources by CAD 150; it is not a tax advantage. A provision is neither an actual return calculation nor proof of payment. No additional sale, cost base, sale tax, non-cash distribution or registered-plan treatment is modeled.

Verification covers cash/investment conservation, an independently priced unit ledger, default monthly steps and partial restart, flat-return equivalence, exhausted-resource shortfalls, interruption clipping, distribution neutrality, outside cash counted once, funding thresholds, zero distributions, all field boundaries, cross-links and four finite accessible figure/table pairs per guide. Original source HTML, hashes, browser captures, detailed logs and release receipts remain local and ignored under `qa/planning-contribution-tax-cash/`.
