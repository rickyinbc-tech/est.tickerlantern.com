## Definitions and historical compatibility

A compatible pair must identify the exact benchmark and be the same publisher’s same-document, same-date, explicitly rolling-12-month series, with no detected definition change. This permits a publisher’s internally paired current/mean series despite undisclosed constituent-level detail; it does not establish comparability with another vendor. Every mean remains an illustrative exit assumption, never fair value or an inevitable reversion. Unknown horizon, differently dated windows or unresolved provider/aggregation/loss-treatment differences block automatic use. A blocked mean remains visible with its original source and date. If no eligible mean exists, the unedited exit follows the active entry assumption; an explicit exit edit wins.

Historical means are the publisher’s reported arithmetic average labels, not medians or ten-year performance returns. Nasdaq explicitly identifies daily observations. Other cited means do not disclose sampling frequency, observation exclusions or exact sampled endpoints; the registry leaves these unknown. A chart’s full date range is not assumed to be the mean’s window. A fresh current multiple cannot refresh an older mean’s date.

## Forward-EPS and distribution timeline

For a rolling-NTM interpretation, the synthetic entry anchor `F0=100/entryPE` represents roughly the first holding year’s earnings. After one holding year, `F1=F0(1+g1)` represents the following earnings period; after n years, `Fn` represents approximately year n+1. The engine applies exactly n growth factors and values each date at `Pt=Mt Ft`. There is no extra growth factor because the entry denominator is already forward looking. For FY/current-year/blended fields, this is a scenario convention rather than an independently verified official index EPS timeline. The application must retain the source’s horizon qualification.

Annual CY/FY/publisher-labelled growth observations do not establish growth of that rolling anchor. The source mode uses them as explicitly approximate scenario assumptions in label order, starting with the next future published label; current-year context is not replayed. Missing years block the calculation rather than repeat a prior forecast. The Amova table’s `Dec-2026` and `Sep-2027` rows are dated EPS-growth scenarios. The original table does not say “Year to”; its exact growth interval is not established. Do not relabel those rows as adjacent full calendar-year forecasts. No interpolation or extension is justified.

Payout mode pays `p×F_(t−1)` once at year end. Yield mode pays `100×y0×F_(t−1)/F0`; the source/assumed yield anchors the first payment only. Subsequent dividend growth follows the scenario EPS path, which is an assumption rather than a sourced distribution schedule. Reinvestment buys extra units at the modeled year-end ex-distribution price and includes the existing unit balance. Cash-held dividends earn0%. No second dividend price deduction or separate buyback yield is added.

Source yield×forwardP/E is not a reported payout ratio. The observations can have incompatible earnings/dividend horizons, providers and dates. Growth, payouts, dilution and buybacks must be economically feasible together; this simple model does not enforce a balance sheet or infer retention×ROE without supporting inputs. Reinvestment can underperform holding cash when prices decline. Annual IRR, terminal cash-held wealth CAGR and reinvested CAGR are distinct measures.
