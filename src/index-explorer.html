How the five-year assumptions work

The model begins with forward earnings. Replaying next calendar year’s growth can count near-term growth twice. Where the source explicitly specifies calendar-year growth and the entry multiple explicitly uses rolling 12-month earnings, a uniform-accrual calendar-to-forward bridge converts relative calendar earnings into five forward-earnings transitions. The recorded valuation date supplies the within-year weight; a publication date is a timing proxy where a separate observation date is undisclosed. A sixth calendar growth assumption completes the terminal forward anchor without adding a sixth holding year.

Other annual, fiscal or forecast-endpoint definitions are not silently converted. Their published figures stay above as context. Each of their five holding years uses that benchmark’s explicit editorial normalized EPS anchor. Every modeled rate is editable. Anchors range from 4.5% to 9%; no evidence establishes these as uniquely correct forecasts. A recession, lower earnings, dividend cuts or multiple compression can give materially worse results.
